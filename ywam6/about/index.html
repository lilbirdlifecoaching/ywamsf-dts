Our mission

Rooted in the way of Jesus, we seek to restore lives, form whole persons, and equip believers to be a compassionate and courageous presence in the world.

Our vision

We envision a Tenderloin where neighbors and the neighborhood are renewed, and where the Church is mobilized to share the love of Christ.

Our values

Four movements that shape us.

They shape our life with God, our posture toward neighbors, and our commitment to multiply disciples.

  1. Love of God

    We draw life from Jesus through worship, Scripture, prayer, and healthy relationships.

    We stay rooted in God.

  2. Love of Neighbor

    We show up for our neighbors through listening, welcome, and shared life.

    We practice compassionate presence.

  3. Humility in Action

    We embrace every task as sacred and serve with joy and humility.

    We look first to the interests of others.

  4. Formation that Multiplies

    We grow as followers of Jesus and help others do the same.

    We form disciples who form disciples.

Our history

Since 1987.

“The city of San Francisco is only 47 square miles in size, but its potential to reach the nations is endless.”
  1. 1987

    YWAM Northern California begins

    John Dawson commissions Dick and Pat Eachus to establish YWAM Northern California from the Southwest Regional headquarters, building on relationships formed by Loren and Darlene Cunningham, the Dawsons and the Eachus family.

  2. 1987–88

    Prayer walking the city

    An office opens, the team develops a system of prayer walking and demographic research, and Cecil and Lillian Cooper transfer the Springs of Living Water property to YWAM.

  3. 1991

    The first DTS

    Our first Discipleship Training School is held at the Bridgemont High School facility.

  4. 1995

    Home on Ellis Street

    Staff move into the Tenderloin Training and Outreach Center at 357 Ellis Street, and Mission Adventures begins.

  5. 1996

    Training in the Tenderloin

    The first DTS at 357 Ellis Street runs in the fall. A second annual school is added in January 2002.

  6. 1998–2004

    Reaching across the city

    The Street Team opens “The Refuge” discipleship house on Girard Street (1998), then The Pharos on Polk Street (2001). The base director moves to Bayview–Hunters Point (1999), and Oakdale Youth House opens there with after-school programs and summer camps (2003). The Street Team returns to Ellis Street in 2004.

  7. 2007

    1,600 on mission

    Mission Adventures hosts more than 1,600 participants in a single year.

  8. 2008

    A full house

    Two DTSs a year, Mission Adventures teams, Oakdale Youth House, the Street Team, a Discipleship Training Program and an Urban Internship.

  9. Today

    Presence that restores

    The Ellis Room, the food pantry, showers and haircuts, Bible study, Pop-Up Church and the DTS, all from the same 100-year-old building on Ellis Street.
