The place behind it

We live on Ellis Street. The door stays open.

Monks used to build their monasteries far from the city. We did the opposite. Our home sits in the middle of the Tenderloin — among neighbors who are unhoused, elderly, newly arrived, recovering, and brilliant — and we pray, eat, work and learn right here.

When it pours, the Ellis Room stays lit all night so friends with nowhere dry to go have somewhere to be. On Sunday mornings, church pops up on the sidewalk over hot chocolate. Every week, the food pantry becomes a place where people are seen by name.

FAQ
What does a typical day look like?

Teaching with the speaker of the week, formational practices like worship and prayer, community activities like small groups and shared meals, local ministry, and free time.

Can I work or go to school during DTS?

We don't recommend it. DTS is time set aside to deepen your relationship with God and experience His heart for people, and your capacity will already be stretched.

Do I need to have my faith figured out?

No. You don't have to figure it out alone. We'd love to hear your story, answer your questions and help you discern your next step.

Is the Tenderloin safe?

It's a real neighborhood with real struggles. You'll never serve alone, and you'll learn from staff who've lived and served here for years. [YWAM SF team: add your specifics here.]

What happens after DTS?

The debrief sets you up for your next season — a job, college, joining YWAM, or wherever God is leading.
